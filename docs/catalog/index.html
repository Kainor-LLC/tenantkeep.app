Check catalog

Every check the product evaluates, by domain. The pass logic, remediation and framework mappings are part of the product; this page lists what is checked and the licence each check needs.

Identity and privileged access (15)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
ID-001Global Administrator count between two and fiveHighnoneEvery Global Administrator is a full tenant takeover away from any phishing kit or malware on that person's machine. Microsoft recommends fewer than five, and two is the floor because the break-glass accounts hold the role. Counts above that usually come from convenience assignments that nobody revisited.
ID-002No permanent privileged assignments outside break-glassHighP2A standing privileged role is a standing target. Eligible assignments mean the role exists only for the hours it is activated, every activation is logged with a reason, and an attacker who lands in the account outside that window gets a normal user. This is what Entra ID P2 is for.
ID-003No administrators synced from on-premises ADHighnoneA synced account's password and status are controlled from on-premises Active Directory. If a domain controller falls, every synced administrator falls with it, and the attacker reaches the cloud without touching it. Microsoft's guidance for protecting Microsoft 365 from on-premises attacks starts with cloud-only privileged accounts.
ID-004Administrator accounts are unlicensed, separate identitiesMediumnoneAn administrator who reads mail and browses with the same account exposes the role to every phishing message and drive-by download. A separate, unlicensed admin account has no mailbox to phish and no Office session to hijack. Licenses on it are the tell that it doubles as a daily account.
ID-005Break-glass accounts exist, are cloud-only and enabledHighnoneWhen MFA, federation or a policy misfire locks every administrator out, the break-glass accounts are the only way back in. They must be cloud-only so an on-premises outage cannot take them, enabled so they work, and excluded from blocking policies so the lockout does not apply to them.
ID-006PIM activation settings on the top rolesHighP2Eligible assignments only help if activation is harder than a click. MFA on activation stops a stolen session from elevating, a duration cap limits how long an activated role lives, and approval on Global Administrator puts a second person between an attacker and the tenant.
ID-007No service principal holds Global AdministratorHighnoneA service principal with a directory role is an administrator with no MFA, no sign-in frequency and usually a secret somewhere in a pipeline. Global Administrator on one of them is the most direct path from a leaked secret to full tenant control.
ID-008User consent limited to admin consent or verified low-impact appsHighnoneConsent phishing asks a user to approve an app that reads their mail and files. If users can consent to any permission, one click gives the attacker a persistent OAuth grant that survives password changes and MFA. Restricting consent closes the door; the workflow keeps legitimate requests moving.
ID-009Admin consent workflow enabled with reviewersMediumnoneOnce user consent is restricted, users need a way to ask. Without the workflow they ask by email or not at all, and administrators grant tenant-wide consent to get them unblocked, which is worse than the user consent that was removed.
ID-010No expired or soon-to-expire application credentialsMediumnoneAn expired credential on a live integration is an outage waiting for the next token refresh. Expired credentials left in place also hide which ones are actually in use. Thirty days is the window in which rotation can be scheduled rather than done in an incident.
ID-011High-privilege application permissions justifiedHighnoneAn application permission applies to every mailbox, site or user in the tenant with no user present. Directory.ReadWrite.All or Mail.ReadWrite on a forgotten app is a complete data exfiltration path that bypasses Conditional Access entirely. Each one should have a name next to it.
ID-012No guests inactive beyond the thresholdMediumP1A guest who has not signed in for months still holds whatever was shared with them, under an identity another organisation controls. Inactive guests are the longest-lived access in most tenants and the least looked at.
ID-013Cross-tenant inbound access reviewed, not default-openMediumnoneThe default cross-tenant settings allow any external user to be invited to any application. That is fine for an organisation that has decided it, and a gap for one that never looked. The check fails on the untouched default so the decision gets made.
ID-014Sign-in and audit logs exported to Log Analytics or a SIEMHighnoneEntra keeps sign-in logs for seven to thirty days depending on license. An incident discovered later than that has no evidence to investigate. Exporting the logs is a one-time setting and the difference between knowing what happened and guessing.
ID-015No high-risk users left unaddressedHighP2A user flagged high risk has leaked credentials or attacker-pattern sign-ins. A week without action means either nobody is watching the report or the user-risk policy is missing. Either way the account is still usable by whoever triggered the detection.

Authentication methods (9)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
AU-001SMS and voice disabled or scoped to a migration groupHighnoneSMS and voice are the weakest second factors: SIM swaps, SS7 interception and social engineering of carriers all defeat them, and they cannot be made phishing-resistant. Leaving them enabled for all users means an attacker who needs a second factor picks the easiest one.
AU-002Authenticator shows app and location contextMediumnoneMFA fatigue attacks spam approvals until the user taps one. Number matching made the tap deliberate; showing the requesting app and the sign-in location tells the user that the request from another country for an app they are not using is not theirs.
AU-003Passkeys enabledHighnonePasskeys are the phishing-resistant method most users can actually adopt: no code to type, nothing to relay, bound to the site. If the method is disabled nothing else in the phishing-resistant story can start.
AU-004Legacy MFA and SSPR policies migratedMediumnoneWhile the migration is in progress, three policies decide which methods a user can register and the effective result is the union of them. Finishing the migration makes the authentication methods policy the one place that matters, which is what every other check here assumes.
AU-005Temporary Access Pass enabled, one-time by defaultLownoneA Temporary Access Pass is how a new user or someone with a lost phone registers a passkey without a password-only window. A reusable pass with a long lifetime is itself a credential worth stealing, so one-time use is the default to keep.
AU-006MFA registered for every memberHighnoneAn MFA policy only protects users who have a method registered; the rest are prompted to register at their next sign-in, which an attacker with the password is happy to do. The registered percentage is the real coverage number behind every MFA policy.
AU-007Passwordless-capable share of members at targetLownonePasswordless-capable users can sign in without a password at all, which removes the credential phishing steals. The number is reported here and compared against the target the organisation sets, since a sensible target depends on where the rollout is.
AU-008Every administrator has a phishing-resistant method registeredCriticalnoneThe phishing-resistant policy for administrators (CA-003) can only be enforced once every administrator has a method that satisfies it. This check is the readiness gate: who still signs in with a push notification or a code that an adversary-in-the-middle kit can relay.
AU-009No users on legacy per-user MFAMediumnonePer-user MFA predates Conditional Access and prompts on every sign-in regardless of risk, device or app. Users on it get MFA fatigue, administrators get no policy control, and it cannot require a phishing-resistant method. It is the tell of a tenant that was set up before 2019 and never revisited.

Conditional Access (15)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
CA-001Legacy authentication blockedCriticalP1Legacy protocols such as basic-auth IMAP, POP and SMTP cannot complete a multifactor prompt, so a password spray or a stolen password signs in without any second factor. Blocking them is the single control that removes most password-only sign-ins from the tenant. A policy in report-only mode or with a broad exclusion group leaves the door open for exactly the accounts an attacker will try.
CA-002MFA required for all usersCriticalP1A tenant-wide MFA policy is the baseline every other control assumes. Per-user MFA and security defaults cover fewer scenarios and cannot be scoped or reported on. Every account excluded from the policy is an account that signs in with a password alone, which is why the check counts a policy as passing only when its exclusions are limited to the break-glass accounts.
CA-003Phishing-resistant authentication for administrator rolesCriticalP1Push notifications and one-time codes can be relayed by an adversary-in-the-middle kit, which captures the session as soon as the administrator approves. Phishing-resistant methods bind the credential to the site, so the relayed prompt fails. Administrators are the accounts those kits are aimed at, and the roles listed here are the ones Microsoft recommends protecting first.
CA-004Risk-based policies for sign-in risk and user riskHighP2Identity Protection scores sign-ins and users on leaked credentials, anonymous networks, impossible travel and attacker infrastructure. Without a policy that acts on the score, the detections are a report nobody reads until after the incident. Requiring MFA on risky sign-ins and a password change on risky users turns the detection into an automatic response.
CA-005Compliant device required for Office 365 on Windows and macOSHighP1, IntuneA stolen password with a valid MFA approval still lands on whatever machine the attacker chooses. Requiring a compliant device ties access to a managed, patched, encrypted endpoint with endpoint protection reporting in, which is the control that keeps session theft and credential phishing from turning into data access.
CA-006No policy left in report-only modeMediumP1Report-only mode exists to measure impact before enforcing. A policy that stays in it for months protects nothing while looking like a control on the policy list. The pileup is a common finding in tenants where a rollout stalled, and it usually hides the policies that would matter most.
CA-007Exclusion groups under the size thresholdHighP1Every member of an exclusion group is outside the policy entirely. Groups created for a migration or a vendor tend to grow and never shrink, and an attacker who lands in one of those accounts faces no MFA at all. The size threshold is the point at which an exclusion stops being an exception and becomes a second, weaker policy.
CA-008No MFA bypass for trusted locationsHighP1Skipping MFA from the office network treats the network as the credential. Anyone on the wireless, a compromised device on the LAN, or a VPN session from a stolen laptop then signs in with a password alone. Zero trust guidance drops the location bypass because the perimeter is the easiest control to get behind.
CA-009Sign-in frequency and non-persistent browser for administratorsMediumP1An administrator session that persists for weeks is a token worth stealing. Limiting how long the session lives and preventing the browser from keeping it caps what a stolen cookie is worth, and it is the control that makes a token replay noticeable rather than silent.
CA-010Token protection in useMediumP1Token protection binds the sign-in token to the device it was issued to, so a session cookie copied from a phished or malware-infected machine stops working elsewhere. It closes the gap that MFA leaves open once the user has already signed in. Client support is still growing, which is why report-only with a date counts here.
CA-011Microsoft-managed policies not silently disabledMediumP1Microsoft creates managed policies in tenants for the controls it considers baseline, such as MFA for administrators and for risky sign-ins, and enables them after a notice period. Disabling one without an equivalent policy removes a control the tenant was about to gain, and the state is easy to miss because the policy stays on the list.
CA-012Break-glass accounts excluded from every blocking policyHighP1A blocking policy that applies to the break-glass accounts can lock every administrator out of the tenant at once, typically during the incident the policy was written for. A policy misfire, an MFA outage or a mistaken location change then has no way back in. Excluding the emergency accounts from every Block is what keeps Conditional Access recoverable.
CA-013No policy references a deleted objectHighP1When a group, user or named location a policy references is deleted, the policy keeps the orphaned id and silently stops covering or excluding whoever it pointed at. The portal shows nothing wrong. The result is a policy that reads correctly and does something different, which is the worst kind of drift.
CA-014No policy left disabled past the thresholdLowP1Disabled policies accumulate from pilots and emergencies and make the policy list hard to read, which is how a reviewer misses the one that matters. A disabled policy can also be re-enabled by mistake with stale assignments. Either it is needed, and should be enabled, or it is not, and should go.
CA-015Policy names follow the naming conventionLowP1A naming convention is what lets a reviewer read a policy list and know what each policy is for without opening it. Tenants whose policies are named by whoever created them tend to have duplicates and gaps nobody noticed. With a pattern in the profile the check enforces it; without one it looks for a prefix-and-number structure across the names, and free-text names are the finding.

Devices (14)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
DEV-001Intune compliance rate at targetHighIntuneThe compliance rate is the fraction of managed devices meeting the policy: encrypted, current, protected, healthy. It is what the compliant-device Conditional Access control enforces, so the number is both a hygiene measure and the size of the population a device-based policy would lock out. The per-platform split is in the facts.
DEV-002No stale Entra devices, or a cleanup rule is onMediumnoneA device object that has not signed in for months still satisfies device-based Conditional Access if someone can use its credential, and it inflates every device count the report relies on. Stale objects are the inventory debt that makes join-type and compliance numbers unreliable.
DEV-003Join type mix: hybrid share within targetLownoneEntra joined, hybrid joined and registered devices get different controls and different attack surfaces. The mix is reported here so the trend is visible across runs; the threshold lets a migration set its own pace.
DEV-004Autopilot share of the Windows fleet at targetLowIntuneAutopilot-provisioned devices arrive Entra joined, enrolled and policy-bound before a user touches them. The share of the Windows fleet that came through Autopilot is a proxy for how much of it was built to the standard rather than migrated into it.
DEV-005BitLocker recovery keys escrowed for every Windows deviceHighnoneA Windows device without an escrowed recovery key is either unencrypted or encrypted with a key nobody holds, and the difference only shows up when the disk needs recovering. Key presence in Entra is the one signal that both encryption happened and the organisation can get the data back.
DEV-006FileVault enabled on every managed MacHighIntuneA lost MacBook without FileVault is a readable disk. Intune reports whether each managed Mac is encrypted; escrow of the recovery key is the second half, so that a locked-out user does not become a wiped machine.
DEV-007No Windows 10 devices left in the fleetHighIntuneAn unsupported operating system gets no security fixes. Every Windows 10 device without ESU is a known-vulnerable endpoint on the network, and the count is the first thing an auditor or an insurer asks about.
DEV-008Apple push certificate and VPP tokens well before expiryHighIntuneWhen the Apple push certificate lapses, every iPhone, iPad and Mac silently stops receiving management. If it is renewed with a different Apple ID, all of them must be re-enrolled by hand. It expires yearly and the person who created it has often left.
DEV-009Personal Windows devices blocked from enrollmentMediumIntuneWith personal Windows enrollment open, anyone with a work account can enroll their home PC, which then counts as managed, may satisfy compliance, and pulls corporate data onto hardware nobody controls. The restriction keeps the managed fleet the organisation's own.
DEV-010Endpoint security policies present and assignedHighIntuneThe five endpoint security policy types are how Intune tells devices to run Defender properly, encrypt the disk, keep the firewall on, block the common attack techniques and report to Defender for Endpoint. A missing type means that control is whatever the device defaulted to.
DEV-011Windows LAPS policy assignedHighIntuneWithout LAPS every Windows device shares the same local administrator password, or has one nobody knows. Either way a single compromised machine gives lateral movement to all of them. LAPS makes each password unique, rotated and retrievable only through an audited read.
DEV-012Defender for Endpoint connector enabled in IntuneHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessThe connector is what lets Intune onboard devices to Defender for Endpoint automatically and use device risk in compliance. Without it, onboarding is manual and a device under attack still reports compliant.
DEV-013Device cleanup rule enabledLowIntuneWithout a cleanup rule, every device that was ever enrolled stays in the console, and the compliance and encryption percentages drift toward meaningless. The rule is a one-time setting that keeps the inventory honest.
DEV-014Linux endpoints enrolled in IntuneMediumIntuneLinux desktops are usually the developers' machines with the most access and the least management. The count enrolled is reported here because zero is the common answer, and at scale zero is a finding.

Endpoint security (25)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
EP-001Security baselines deployed: Windows, Defender for Endpoint, EdgeMediumIntuneThe baselines carry several hundred hardening settings Microsoft maintains. Without them a tenant depends on whatever a custom profile happened to include. Version currency is not checked here yet.
EP-002BitLocker policy: required, XTS-AES 256, silent, TPM, keys escrowedHighIntuneA lost laptop with no encryption is a breach; with encryption and no escrowed key it is a brick with no way back. Silent enablement is what makes the policy reach every device rather than the ones whose users clicked through.
EP-003Windows Firewall on for domain, private and public profilesHighIntuneThe host firewall is what stands between a laptop on hotel Wi-Fi and lateral movement. A profile left unmanaged can be turned off by any local admin or a legacy GPO.
EP-004Windows LAPS: backup to Entra, password reset after useHighIntuneA shared local administrator password is the oldest lateral movement path there is. LAPS gives every device its own, stored where it can be audited and rotated after each use.
EP-005Defender Antivirus: real-time, cloud, PUA, sample submission, scheduled scanHighIntuneDefender's defaults are good; a policy makes them stick and makes them auditable. Cloud protection is where the detection logic actually lives, so a device with it off is running last year's engine.
EP-006Tamper protection onHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessMalware that lands with admin rights first turns off the antivirus. Tamper protection takes that option away from everything except Intune and the Defender portal.
EP-007Network protection in block modeMediumMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessNetwork protection blocks outbound connections to known malicious hosts from any process, not only the browser. In audit mode it reports and lets the traffic through.
EP-008Attack surface reduction rules: every rule blocking, none offHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessASR rules stop the techniques phishing payloads use: Office spawning processes, scripts launching downloaded executables, credential theft from LSASS. Audit mode generates events and stops nothing. The effective state per device is a later collector; this is the policy.
EP-009No ASR-only exclusions, or each documentedMediumMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessAn exclusion turns the rules off for a path. Attackers know the common ones.
EP-010SmartScreen on in the shell, override blockedMediumIntuneSmartScreen checks downloaded files and installers against reputation before they run. The override setting is what stops a user from clicking Run anyway.
EP-011Office macros from the internet blockedHighIntuneMicrosoft blocks internet macros by default since 2022, but a user or an old GPO can turn that back on. A policy makes it enforced.
EP-012Windows Hello for Business enabledMediumIntuneHello for Business replaces the password at the device with a key bound to the TPM. It is the phishing-resistant method most users can have today.
EP-013Cloud Kerberos trust configured for hybrid sign-inMediumIntuneWithout cloud Kerberos trust, hybrid-joined devices need certificates or key trust to reach on-premises resources after a Hello sign-in, which is where most hybrid Hello deployments stall.
EP-014Update rings with a quality update deadline, feature update profile assignedMediumIntuneA ring without a deadline is a suggestion. The deadline is what gets the patch installed on the laptop that never reboots.
EP-015macOS FileVault policy with recovery key escrowHighIntuneSame reasoning as BitLocker: the policy makes encryption universal and the escrow makes recovery possible. Rotation after a reveal is an effective-state check for later.
EP-016macOS hardening: Gatekeeper, firewall, SIP requiredMediumIntuneGatekeeper and SIP are on by default and can be turned off by any admin user, which on most Macs is the user. The compliance policy is what notices.
EP-017Platform SSO deployed on macOSLowIntunePlatform SSO gives the Mac a device-bound credential the way Hello does on Windows, and makes the local account follow the Entra password. The mode in use is not recorded yet.
EP-018Mobile: app protection assigned, jailbroken and rooted devices blockedHighIntuneApp protection is what keeps company data inside managed apps on a phone the company does not own. Jailbreak detection is the compliance signal Conditional Access needs for the corporate ones. The check applies whenever a mobile platform is in scope, whatever the stated BYOD stance: phones reach mail either way.
EP-019Defender for Endpoint onboarded on the Intune fleetHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessPolicy state said every device gets the EDR policy; this is the share that actually reports to Defender. The gap between the two is the finding the spec asks for. Joined on the Entra device id.
EP-020Defender sensors healthy: none inactive or impaired over 7 daysHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessAn onboarded device whose sensor is silent is unprotected and still counts as covered in every dashboard. Seven days is Microsoft's own inactive threshold.
EP-021Defender Antivirus active on every deviceHighMDE1, MDE2, MDEBusinessPassive mode means another product is the antivirus and Defender only observes. Disabled means nothing is. Both are invisible in policy state, which says the antivirus settings are configured.
EP-022Attack surface reduction rules effective on the fleetHighMDE2The policy check says the rules are set to block; the secure configuration assessment says which devices are actually enforcing each rule. Both views are in the report.
EP-023Tamper protection and real-time protection effective on the fleetHighMDE2Effective state for the controls that matter most: tamper protection, real-time protection, cloud-delivered protection, network protection, as the devices report them rather than as the policy says.
EP-024No critical vulnerabilities open beyond 30 daysHighMDE2Critical means a working exploit path. Thirty days after publication is past any reasonable patch cycle. Defender Vulnerability Management counts them per device; the summary counts distinct CVEs and devices.
EP-025No Defender incidents open beyond 7 days without an ownerMediumE5, MDE2An unassigned incident older than a week is one nobody is working. It says more about the operation than any single detection.

Email security (21)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
EM-001EOP preset security policy assigned to everyoneHighnonePreset policies carry Microsoft's recommended anti-spam, anti-malware and anti-phishing settings and update as the recommendations change. A tenant on hand-tuned custom policies drifts; one on the preset with no scope restrictions is protected by default.
EM-002Defender for Office 365 preset policy assigned to everyoneHighMDO1, MDO2The MDO half of the preset is a separate rule. Tenants often enable the EOP preset and never the MDO one, which leaves Safe Links and Safe Attachments on custom policies or off.
EM-003Anti-phishing: spoof intelligence on and DMARC honouredHighnoneSpoof intelligence catches senders using your domains or trusted partners' domains without authorisation. Honouring DMARC applies the sender's own published policy instead of the tenant's spam verdict.
EM-004Impersonation protection for executives and owned domainsMediumMDO1, MDO2Business email compromise starts with a lookalike display name or domain. Impersonation protection needs the names it should protect; a policy with an empty list protects nobody.
EM-005Safe Links and Safe Attachments on for mail, Teams and OfficeHighMDO1, MDO2URL rewriting and detonation at click time, plus attachment detonation, are the controls that stop the payload after the lure got through. Each needs its own policy switch.
EM-006Zero-hour auto purge for spam, phish and malwareMediumnoneZAP retroactively moves messages already delivered once the verdict changes. Off, a message that was clean at delivery and malicious an hour later stays in the inbox.
EM-007Quarantine: admin-only release for high-confidence phish, user notifications onMediumnoneA user who can release high-confidence phish to themselves defeats the filter. Notifications let users request release of legitimate mail without an admin.
EM-008Automatic external forwarding blockedHighnoneAttackers who get a mailbox set a forward and read quietly for months. Blocking it at the tenant level stops both inbox rules and mailbox forwarding from leaving the tenant.
EM-009Connection filter IP allow list emptyMediumnoneAn IP on the allow list skips spam filtering entirely. Legacy entries for a long-gone gateway or a shared cloud range are an open door.
EM-010No unscoped SCL -1 transport rulesMediumnoneA rule that sets SCL -1 on all mail from a domain or a scope turns filtering off for anything that claims to come from it. Spoofed senders pass straight through.
EM-011Enhanced Filtering on every partner inbound connectorMediumnoneWith a gateway in front, every message arrives from the gateway's IP, SPF fails and spoof intelligence is blind. Enhanced Filtering restores the original connecting IP.
EM-012DKIM signing enabled on every custom domainHighnoneDKIM proves the message body was sent by the domain owner's infrastructure and survives forwarding, which SPF does not. DMARC alignment on outbound mail depends on it.
EM-013DMARC at the target policy with reportingHighnoneDMARC tells receivers what to do with mail that fails SPF and DKIM alignment. p=none is monitoring only; without rua nobody sees the reports.
EM-014SPF valid, under the lookup limit, hard failHighnoneSPF names the servers allowed to send for the domain. ~all asks receivers to be lenient; more than ten lookups makes the record permanently fail evaluation. The lookup count here is the top-level record only.
EM-015MTA-STS and TLS-RPT publishedLownoneMTA-STS makes senders refuse to deliver to the domain without a valid TLS connection, closing the downgrade. TLS-RPT is how failures get reported.
EM-016No mailboxes forwarding externallyHighnoneMailbox-level forwarding is the second place an attacker persists after inbox rules. Forwarding to a recipient object is listed for verification because the target can be an external contact.
EM-017Legacy protocols off: SMTP AUTH org-wide, POP and IMAP per mailboxMediumnoneBasic authentication for these protocols is retired, but the protocols themselves still work with modern auth and bypass the clients Conditional Access expects. SMTP AUTH is the one that still accepts a password.
EM-018Shared mailboxes blocked from sign-inMediumnoneThe account behind a shared mailbox is created enabled with a random password. Enabled, it is a licence-free, usually MFA-free account anyone who resets the password can use.
EM-019Mailbox auditing on and the unified audit log enabledHighnoneWithout the audit log there is no investigation: who read, moved or forwarded what. Mailbox auditing is on by default only while the organisation switch stays off.
EM-020Attack simulation campaign in the last 90 daysLowMDO2Users are the last filter. A tenant with the licence and no campaigns has the tooling and not the practice.
EM-021ARC trusted sealers configured for the gatewayMediumnoneA gateway that modifies mail breaks DKIM and SPF for the next hop. ARC lets the gateway vouch for the original results; the tenant has to trust the sealer for that to count.

Collaboration and oversharing (14)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
CL-001Tenant external sharing level below AnyoneHighnoneAnyone links need no sign-in, so the link is the credential: forwarded, pasted into a chat, indexed from a document, it keeps working. Guest sharing keeps an identity on every access.
CL-002Anonymous links disabled or expiring within the thresholdHighnoneAn Anyone link without an expiry lives until someone finds and removes it. Expiry puts a bound on the exposure of every link ever created.
CL-003Default sharing link is Specific people with ViewMediumnoneThe default is what most people pick. Specific people with View makes the common case the safe case; Edit for everyone in the organisation makes every share an overshare.
CL-004Sharing domain restrictions in placeMediumnoneThe profile says external collaboration is locked or governed. Without a domain list, governed means a guest from any domain, including personal mailboxes.
CL-005Everyone, Everyone except external users and All Users hidden from the people pickerMediumnoneA site shared with Everyone except external users is shared with every account in the tenant, and with Copilot. CL-006Guest access expires within the thresholdMediumnoneGuests outlive projects. Expiration is the only automatic removal SharePoint and OneDrive offer without an access review licence.
CL-007No ownerless sites or Microsoft 365 groupsMediumnoneNobody can approve access, review sharing or delete an ownerless site. They accumulate when the owner leaves and the offboarding does not reassign them.
CL-008Restricted access control enabledLowSAMRestricted access control binds a site to a security group regardless of how the content was shared. It is the fix for oversharing that cannot be unwound link by link.
CL-009Microsoft 365 group expiration policy enabledLowP1Teams and sites created for a quarter stay forever without it. Expiration with automatic renewal on activity removes only the ones nobody uses.
CL-010Teams federation limited to an allow list, or offMediumnoneOpen federation lets any Teams tenant start a chat with your users, which is the delivery path for Teams phishing. An allow list keeps the partners and drops the rest.
CL-011Teams consumer and Skype accounts blockedMediumnonePersonal accounts have no tenant behind them to hold accountable. Blocking them removes the cheapest impersonation path.
CL-012Teams guest access governed or disabledMediumnoneGuests in Teams reach the files behind every channel they are in. Governed means they leave on a schedule unless someone renews them.
CL-013Meeting lobby for external and anonymous participantsMediumnoneEveryone bypassing the lobby means a leaked meeting link is an open door. Anonymous join itself is often needed; the lobby is what makes it safe.
CL-014Third-party Teams apps restricted to an allow listLownoneA Teams app is an OAuth grant plus a surface inside every chat. Allowing all third-party apps lets any user consent to any of them. (verify) app centric management replaces this policy on newer tenants.

Copilot readiness (18)

IDCheckSeverityLicenceWhat it means
CP-001Audit log on, Copilot interactions retainedHighnoneWithout the audit log there is no record of what Copilot read or who asked. It is the first hard gate because every later question about Copilot exposure needs it.
CP-002SharePoint Advanced Management availableHighnoneThe oversharing baseline, restricted access control and restricted content discovery all sit behind this licence. Without it the stopgaps are not available.
CP-003Oversharing baseline run within 90 daysHighSAMThe baseline is the list of sites Copilot would surface too widely. Everything after this gate works that list.
